Simulation notes

How the simulation works

Facts retained from the case

The club has $1,000 and 50 regular members. All 50 members want to enter the town tournament. The municipal department has a 20% chance of checking whether the $100 registration fee was paid. The reduced library has a 20% chance of omitting books that members requested.

Rules added for the game

The club needs 15 clocks, which accommodate 30 simultaneous players. Peak attendance varies between 18 and 30 members. If the damaged computer is not repaired, morale declines and there is a 40% chance that half of the club's remaining members leave.

If funded, grandmaster coaching has a 55% chance of attracting substantial media attention; the digital package has a 45% chance of recruiting 4–8 new members; and pizza has a 55% chance of recruiting 3–6 new members. These are game assumptions rather than facts supplied by the original case.

Candidate Master and Grandmaster modes

Candidate Master preserves the original, comparatively forgiving outcome rules. Grandmaster assesses the same decisions using a more demanding year-end standard that considers performance across membership, morale, committee support, public reputation, and presidential standing.

In Grandmaster mode, the $100 computer repair cost is a preliminary estimate. If you fund the repair, there is a 45% chance that the estimate is accurate. There is a 25% chance that replacing the power supply adds $50, a 20% chance that replacing the storage drive and paying for additional labour adds $100, and a 10% chance that damage to the motherboard adds $150. An unspent reserve can cover this increase. If the reserve is insufficient, hurried reductions elsewhere damage morale, committee support, reputation, and presidential standing.

In Grandmaster mode, an avoidable risk may also affect the year-end assessment even when its most serious consequence does not occur. For example, if the club avoids a municipal check after leaving the registration fee unpaid, the decision will remain visible in the year-end accounts.

How the result should be interpreted

The game assesses the decision separately from the outcome. A fortunate outcome does not make an imprudent decision sound, and an unfortunate outcome does not necessarily make a defensible decision unreasonable.

Green result cards indicate that the club performed strongly on that measure. Amber cards indicate a mixed result in which important concerns remain. Red cards indicate that the result presents a serious problem.
